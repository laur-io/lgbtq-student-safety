Understanding harm and accessing resources

What counts as harm?

Harm can come in many forms, so it is important for an individual to understand how to define what they may be going through. That makes seeking help more specific to an individual's situation.

Negative treatment based on sex or gender

Negative treatment of any kind due to sex or gender, in a private or public setting. This can come from any unwelcome behavior.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Misgendering
  • Stereotyping
  • Unwanted sexual advances
  • Invasive questions
  • Exclusion

Physical abuse

The intentional and willful use of force that causes (or risks) body pain or injury.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Hitting
  • Kicking
  • Objects thrown at the victim
  • Weapons used on the victim

Emotional abuse

Repeated, nonphysical behaviors used to frighten an individual.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Name calling
  • Gaslighting
  • Silent treatment
  • Guilt tripping
  • Limiting contact with outside parties (friends and family)

Intimidation

The act of making an individual feel afraid or isolated through aggressive behavior.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Threats
  • Coercion
  • Yelling
  • The feeling of always needing to check behind yourself

Online harm

In this era of technology, it is important to understand that harm can come from both in-person and online interactions.

Cyberbullying: unwanted texts, pictures, or sharing of private information.

How can harm affect someone?

Harm can have negative effects on an individual. The effects can be physical (external or internal injuries) or emotional (anxiety, sadness, guilt, shame, or anger).

The effects can also be long lasting, touching an individual's mental health, physical well-being, relationships, and ability to function day to day.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Depression
  • Avoidance
  • Fatigue
  • Unhealthy coping habits
  • Difficulty trusting others
  • Difficulty maintaining close relationships
  • Self-esteem issues

Effects of harm may not show up right after an incident. They can develop days, weeks, months, or even years later.

What can you do?

  • Find a safe space to decompress
  • Talk to someone you trust
  • After anything physical, assess for any injuries, and if it is safe, seek medical attention
  • Go easy on yourself and allow yourself to feel your emotions, with no timeline
  • Contact a resource center
  • File a report

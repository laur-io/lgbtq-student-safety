Your options matter.

A guide for LGBTQ+ students at Sacramento State who have experienced harassment or discrimination, and for friends who want to help. It combines campus resources, reporting options, confidentiality information, and community-based support in one place so students can better understand their choices and find help.

What this website covers

Our website provides a safe space for LGBTQ+ students to find resources after experiencing harassment or discrimination, including how to find support, understand confidentiality, and explore reporting options. It also provides information about community-based support, peer care, and alternatives to relying only on formal university systems. You do not have to report anything to use this site. Nothing you do here is tracked or recorded.

Questions we answer

What makes a resource trustworthy?

A trustworthy resource clearly explains what support it provides, how it protects your privacy, and what may happen after you contact it. It should be accessible, respectful of your identity, and honest about its limitations.

Read more

Who should report harm?

No one should feel pressured to report before they are ready. Students can choose whether to report, seek confidential support first, or ask someone they trust to help them understand their options.

Read more

What risks can reporting create?

Reporting may create concerns about privacy, retaliation, being outed, institutional procedures, or losing control over who receives your information. Understanding these risks can help students make informed choices.

Read more

What options exist outside CSUS?

Support can also come from trusted friends, LGBTQ+ organizations, peer networks, community groups, mutual aid, counseling services, and off-campus advocacy organizations.

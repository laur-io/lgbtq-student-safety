Reporting, confidentiality, and institutional risks

You have options

Harassment or discrimination based on sexual orientation, gender identity, and/or gender expression can affect the safety, confidence, and academic achievement of LGBTQ+ students.

However, students do not need to report an incident at the time. They can first get emotional support and information about available resources and the reporting process before taking action.

What is confidentiality?

Confidentiality refers to safeguarding the personal information you share with a support provider. It is not routinely shared without your consent, except as required by law or for safety reasons.

Reporting options

Sacramento State offers several reporting and support options.

  • Office of Equal Opportunity (EOE): report discrimination, harassment, or retaliation at (916) 278-5700 or equalopportunity@csus.edu.
  • Title IX: deals with discrimination and harassment based on sex.
  • Confidential assistance: contact Counseling Services at (916) 278-6461 or the WEAVE Confidential Campus Advocate at (916) 278-5850.
  • LGBTQ+ community support: the PRIDE Student Resource Center at Sacramento State.

Before you share details: it is university policy that most university employees must report certain disclosures of sexual misconduct and sexual harassment to EOE. Ask whether the employee will keep your information confidential before giving identifying information.

What happens after making a report?

Once a report is received, EOE determines whether the incident violates university policy, considers the complainant's wishes, and evaluates possible campus safety issues. Students can ask questions, learn what assistance is offered, and invite an advisor to meetings. Depending on the circumstances, the university can provide an informal resolution or a formal investigation.

Possible risks and questions to ask before reporting

  • Would reporting make others learn about my LGBTQ+ status?
  • Who will have access to my personal information?
  • Does reporting have any implications for my academic or living setup?
  • Is it possible to back out of the process?
  • What support and protection are available?

Why formal systems may not be enough

Although they ensure accountability, institutional reporting systems do not necessarily support safety or justice. Ahmed (2021) elaborates that complaints might be met with institutional barriers, delays, and dismissal. Similarly, Spade (2015) highlights how administrative structures and legal safeguards can reproduce disparities for marginalized groups. In response to traditional institutional reactions, Boggs (2019) suggests developing collective responses to safety, accountability, and community support.

Therefore, LGBTQ+ student safety needs to be ensured beyond reporting models. Sacramento State should strengthen an inclusive campus culture, anonymous support, peer networks, and effective safeguarding against discrimination and retaliation.
